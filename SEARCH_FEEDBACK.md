# Improve job searches from application feedback

NAVI uses your private records to refine search recommendations. This is an instruction-based review process, not model training, automatic account access, or a background service.

Keep completed reviews and application history in your own private files. This public guide contains no candidate records.

## When to review

Before the next job search, read the current approved profile and the latest available feedback review. When new choices, employer feedback, or outcomes arrive, reconcile the affected records and assess whether the search strategy needs adjustment. If the information is unavailable, use the approved profile and identify the missing evidence.

A review can happen during a normal chat. A recurring background review requires a separately configured and confirmed schedule.

## Separate the signals

| Signal | What it can tell you | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Candidate chooses or skips a role, with a reason | Interest, preferences, or an explicit constraint | Employer interest or probability of an offer |
| Application submitted | Where effort was invested | That the role was a strong fit |
| Automated receipt | Submission acknowledgment | Human review or progress |
| Recruiter screen or interview invitation | Employer interest for that specific application | Why the invitation occurred |
| Explicit employer feedback | What the employer actually communicated | A broader explanation the employer did not provide |
| Rejection without explanation | An outcome for the matched role | A qualification gap, resume defect, or reason |
| Candidate's interview reflection | Interest in the actual work and perceived strengths/gaps | An objective assessment of performance |
| Pending application or silence | Outcome remains unknown | Rejection or evidence that a role category should be excluded |

Preserve the message source, application key, event date, and date checked. Keep separate applications at the same company distinct. Label candidate reports as such.

## Compare responsibilities, not just titles

Group roles by responsibilities, industry, seniority, work arrangement, and required skills. Record which claims reflect proven experience, transferable experience, or learning.

Look for useful neighboring titles from actual postings. A title alone should not cause inclusion or exclusion. Continue verifying open official postings, geographic eligibility, and pay evidence.

Prior applications are selected examples, not a complete picture of the job market. Do not assume that roles never previously applied to are poor opportunities.

## Review the evidence fairly

1. Deduplicate applications before counting.
2. Report unique submitted applications, those reaching a screen/interview, explicit rejections, and pending/unknown outcomes separately.
3. State the observation window and category definitions. Recent applications have had less time to receive responses; compare similar-age groups where possible.
4. If reporting a rate, name the event and denominator. For example, applications with at least one interview invitation divided by unique submitted applications in the same cohort. Do not count multiple interviews for one role as multiple successful applications.
5. Do not silently drop pending applications or treat them as rejected. Show counts and acknowledge incomplete observation.
6. Treat small groups as tentative evidence. No fixed small count proves a pattern.
7. Consider source, referrals, timing, resume version, seniority, and candidate preferences as possible differences. An association does not establish what caused an interview.
8. Never invent a match probability, interview probability, or quantified improvement from these records.

## Propose a focused adjustment

For each suggestion, show:

- The responsibility cluster or search term to emphasize, reduce, or explore.
- Supporting application keys, sources, dates, and counts.
- Candidate preference evidence separately from employer response evidence.
- Missing evidence and alternative explanations.
- Confidence described plainly: tentative or supported by repeated observations.
- A small change to try and what future evidence would support keeping it.

Do not change salary minimums, geography, work arrangement requirements, excluded employers, work authorization, or other hard constraints unless the candidate explicitly approves the change. Do not weaken truthful resume claims to match a pattern.

Present new strategy changes for approval. Once approved, save a dated private profile/review version and use it in future searches. Candidate approval can be given in ordinary language. If saving is unavailable, provide the exact update and identify the manual step.

## Use approved learning in the next search

Prioritize roles combining verified strengths, candidate interest, and promising observed responses. Include a clearly labeled exploration option when useful and within the candidate's approved constraints; do not let a narrow history eliminate adjacent opportunities.

For each recommended posting, explain why it fits, which approved learning influenced it, gaps, unknowns, and the official source/check date. Check prior applications before recommending another submission.

Ask for a brief choice reason when the candidate selects or skips a role, without turning every recommendation into a lengthy questionnaire.

## Fictional example

Suppose four operations applications produced two screens, while two product applications are still pending. The candidate also prefers cross-functional operations work.

A reasonable proposal is to emphasize operations responsibilities in the next search while retaining relevant product opportunities. State the small sample and unequal outcome maturity. Do not say operations has a proven higher success rate or product roles are unsuitable.

## Checks after every review

Confirm no personal information was published, no constraint was silently changed, no pending application was relabeled as rejected, and no unverified write or scheduled task was claimed. Keep the approved learning available as a private source for the next session.
